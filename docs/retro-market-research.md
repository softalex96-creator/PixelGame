# Comparable gaming storefront research

This research informs interaction and information-architecture choices only. PixelGame will not copy third-party branding, copy, product media, or layouts.

| Reference | Relevant product scope | Transferable pattern for PixelGame | Avoid copying |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [G2A](https://www.g2a.com/) | Keys, in-game items, subscriptions, gift cards | Search-first header, a short discovery taxonomy, compact product metadata (platform, delivery mode, region), plus dedicated bestseller and bundle rails | Black-and-purple visual system, seller-marketplace claims, third-party titles and promotion framing |
| [Eneba](https://www.eneba.com/) | Games, game points/top-ups, e-cards and gift cards | Horizontal category strip, campaign-led hero, “recommended” rail, visible price/discount/cashback data, and a clear search placeholder that names top-ups | Purple brand palette, campaign artwork, category copy and commercial offers |
| [G2G](https://www.g2g.com/) | Coins, top-ups, game items, accounts and services | Begin with confidence signals, then make game-first category pages expose available product types; use product-type chips as fast filters | Marketplace seller claims, review volumes, seller/taking-payment mechanics |
| [DMarket](https://dmarket.com/) | Skins and in-game inventories | A persistent inventory-oriented navigation and collection-led merchandising make skins feel tangible; favourite and price-target concepts make high-consideration browsing easier | Trading/exchange promises, Steam-linked inventory flow, game-item media and dark-green brand treatment |
| [Gameflip](https://gameflip.com/en) | Games, gift cards and game items/skins | Its public positioning validates a mixed digital-goods taxonomy; PixelGame will adopt the clear separation of goods by type rather than treating all offers as currency | Marketplace resale proposition, trading flow and branded content |
| [Skinport](https://skinport.com/) | Game skins | Product cards expose the few attributes buyers need to compare an item: item class, condition, rarity-like qualifier, reference price and current price; campaign hero leads into focused inventory browse | CS2 item names/media, price benchmarks, trading language and platform-specific taxonomy |
| [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/) | Mods, collections and modding guidance | Separate game, mod, collection and community paths; position installation support and update visibility as part of the product—not as a footnote | Creator rewards, platform logos, mod downloads and proprietary collections |
| [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/) | Mods, add-ons and creator ecosystem | A game-first selector can frame discovery; mod cards should make scope, freshness and install readiness obvious; a small “how to use” path builds confidence | Its claims, creator metrics, managed-download/app model and third-party game assets |
| [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/) | Mods, add-ons, files and articles | A compact content taxonomy can place “mods” alongside “articles” and “files”; recent-release signaling gives an editorial feed a useful rhythm | Its community identity, listings and archival visual treatment |
| [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/) | Game guides, walkthroughs and interactive maps | Guides benefit from an explicit update date, a recognisable “guide” label, game-first routing and optional interactive-map utility; PixelGame can package guides as clear digital reference cards | Its newsroom/editorial branding, article content and external game guides |

## Direction emerging for PixelGame

PixelGame should use **search plus arcade-cabinet category selection** rather than an undifferentiated product grid. Every product card will expose its product type—currency, skin, mod or guide—alongside its game world, delivery format and local-preview status. The eventual 1980s treatment will use original pixel-scanline mechanics and a curated, legible catalogue rather than imitating either reference’s aesthetic.

## Ten-site synthesis

The ten reviewed references cover general digital marketplace discovery (G2A, Eneba, G2G, Gameflip), skins (DMarket, Skinport), mods (Nexus Mods, CurseForge, ModDB) and guides (Gamepressure). The reusable conclusion is deliberately simple: **one search, four product-type doors, game-first browsing, and metadata that explains delivery/readiness before price**. PixelGame will retain its transparent local-preview checkout, avoid resale/escrow assertions, and distinguish every invented product from a real third-party game item.
